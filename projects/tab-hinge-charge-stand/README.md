# TAB Hinge & Charge Stand — Concept Development

**Blue Sky Solar Racing · Solar vehicle body integration · Started November 2026**  
**Project phase: problem definition, functional requirements and preliminary benchmarking.**  
*No final mechanism, detailed CATIA design, validated kinematics, fabricated prototype or FEA results yet.*

## Problem and engineering context

The **Top Aerobody (TAB)** carries the vehicle's solar array and must open relative to the **Bottom Aerobody (BAB)** to orient the solar cells for charging and permit access. This is not simply a hinge selection problem: the TAB is a large, comparatively flexible structure that must remain supported and controlled throughout movement.

The proposed hinge and charge-stand system must balance:

- **Motion and packaging:** Open and close without contacting the BAB, roll cage, rear fin, solar array or other structures. The TAB and BAB begin in contact at their intended closed interface.
- **Structural behaviour:** Resist unintended TAB bending, twisting and lateral motion. Avoid concentrating loads in composite mounting areas or hardpoints close to an edge.
- **Ergonomics:** Reduce the lifting effort, personnel and time needed, particularly where hinge leverage or moments are unfavorable at small opening angles.
- **Solar performance:** Choose a TAB/BAB split line compatible with solar coverage, stiffness, aerodynamics and an achievable opening path.
- **Maintenance:** Keep fasteners, hardpoints and charge supports accessible during installation and servicing.

## Current contribution — early-phase engineering definition

The work so far consists of **consolidating and drafting functions, objectives and constraints**, beginning competitor/previous-generation **benchmarking**, and mapping interfaces that will influence later concept generation. The draft requirements were prepared for team-lead review; they should not be treated as an approved final specification.

### Representative functional requirements

| Topic | Working requirement / investigation |
| --- | --- |
| Controlled opening | Enable controlled TAB opening and closing, while constraining unintended degrees of freedom |
| Charging positions | Reach **90°** and support adjustments from **30° to 90°** in increments of **3° or finer** (existing team requirement) |
| Clearance | Ensure no interference through the prescribed motion range, particularly with the roll cage, rear fin and BAB |
| TAB/BAB split | Establish a split line that balances array effectiveness, aerobody manufacturability, stiffness and hinge clearance |
| Hinge arrangement | Evaluate the number, locations and spacing of hinges; two-hinge solutions are a reference, not yet a selected design |
| Charge stands | Investigate tool-free length adjustment, stationary load capacity and access for charging operations |
| TAB removal | Preserve the team target of removal by **six or fewer people in under five minutes**, using standard tools |

These are **design inputs**, not demonstrated test outcomes. Some requirements (especially single-stand loading and tool-free adjustment) still require team confirmation.

### Preliminary benchmarking questions

Early research is comparing previous Blue Sky Solar and other teams' systems by TAB/BAB split-line reasoning, hinge mechanism family (e.g., four-bar versus six-bar), number and layout of supports, clearance path, charge-stand adjustment, and ease of assembly.

A previous-generation example used a four-bar mechanism. Research notes suggest its split line was determined primarily by composite manufacturing draft analysis, which motivates considering kinematics, stiffness and solar coverage more explicitly for the next-generation system. This is a **benchmarking observation**, not a decision to reuse that geometry.
